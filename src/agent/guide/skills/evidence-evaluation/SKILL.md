# Evidence Evaluation Skill

## Objective
Assess whether retrieved evidence is relevant, credible, and sufficient to answer the research question.

## Procedure

1. Evaluate each source for:
   - Relevance to the research question.
   - Publication date and currency.
   - Source credibility and provenance.
   - Methodological quality, where details are available.
   - Direct support for the claims being considered.

2. Compare sources to identify:
   - Consistent findings.
   - Contradictory conclusions.
   - Methodological limitations.
   - Missing evidence or underrepresented perspectives.

3. Determine whether the available evidence sufficiently addresses the research question.

4. If significant gaps remain, perform another focused search when permitted.

## Evidence Rules

- Do not treat search-result ranking as proof of credibility.
- Distinguish peer-reviewed research from preprints and general web sources.
- Do not assume a publication is peer-reviewed without evidence.
- Do not infer methodological quality from an abstract alone.
- Treat source snippets as incomplete evidence.
- Never cite a source for claims it does not support.
- Explicitly acknowledge conflicting or insufficient evidence.

## Output

For each important finding, identify:
- The supporting source ID.
- The finding supported by that source.
- Relevant limitations or uncertainty.

Use the evaluation to guide further research and final synthesis.
